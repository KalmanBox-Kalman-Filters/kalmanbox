# Advanced Filters — EKF, UKF, Square-Root, Information, EnKF

## Overview

The standard linear-Gaussian Kalman filter assumes (i) linear transition and
observation maps and (ii) numerically well-behaved state covariances. Real
problems routinely violate one or both assumptions: the dynamics may be
nonlinear (pendulums, target tracking in polar coordinates, chaotic
geophysical models), the state dimension may be very large (numerical weather
prediction), or the covariance updates may lose positive-definiteness in
finite precision (long horizons, near-singular observations). This examples
group covers the five most widely used extensions:

| Filter | Replaces | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **EKF**  | linearisation of $f$ and $h$ at the current mean | dynamics/observations are smooth and mildly nonlinear |
| **UKF**  | deterministic sigma-point propagation (no Jacobian) | nonlinearities are strong or Jacobians are awkward |
| **SQRT** | propagates $S$ with $P = S S^\top$ instead of $P$ | long horizons, near-singular $H$, embedded systems |
| **IF**   | propagates $Y = P^{-1}$, $y = P^{-1} \hat x$ | high-dimensional state, decentralised sensor fusion |
| **EnKF** | Monte Carlo ensemble approximating $P$ | very high state dimension (e.g. $10^6$), strong nonlinearity |

The notebooks (added in subphases F6.2–F6.5) exercise these filters on three
canonical synthetic datasets generated by
[`data/generate_nonlinear.py`](data/generate_nonlinear.py): the nonlinear
pendulum, 2-D target tracking with range/bearing observations, and the Lorenz
63 chaotic ODE.

## Filter equations

We use the following nonlinear state-space formulation throughout:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{k} &= f(x_{k-1}) + w_{k-1}, \quad w_{k-1} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q)\\
y_{k} &= h(x_{k}) + v_{k}, \qquad\;\; v_{k} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R)
\end{aligned}
$$

with $\hat x_{k|k-1}, P_{k|k-1}$ the predicted mean and covariance,
$\hat x_{k|k}, P_{k|k}$ the filtered (posterior) mean and covariance, and
$K_k$ the Kalman gain.

### Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)

Linearise $f, h$ around the current best estimate via Jacobians
$F_k = \partial f/\partial x|_{\hat x_{k-1|k-1}}$ and
$H_k = \partial h/\partial x|_{\hat x_{k|k-1}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat x_{k|k-1} &= f(\hat x_{k-1|k-1}) \\
P_{k|k-1}     &= F_k P_{k-1|k-1} F_k^\top + Q \\
S_k &= H_k P_{k|k-1} H_k^\top + R \\
K_k &= P_{k|k-1} H_k^\top S_k^{-1} \\
\hat x_{k|k} &= \hat x_{k|k-1} + K_k (y_k - h(\hat x_{k|k-1})) \\
P_{k|k}      &= (I - K_k H_k) P_{k|k-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Cheap and unbiased to first order, but the Jacobians can mis-represent
strong curvature and the filter has no built-in mechanism to detect
divergence.

### Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF)

Pick $2n+1$ deterministic *sigma points* $\mathcal{X}^{(i)}$ with weights
$W^{(i)}_m, W^{(i)}_c$ that match the first two moments of
$\mathcal{N}(\hat x, P)$ (Julier & Uhlmann 2004 use the scaling parameters
$\alpha, \beta, \kappa$ to control higher-order accuracy):

$$
\mathcal{X}^{(0)} = \hat x, \quad
\mathcal{X}^{(i)} = \hat x \pm \left(\sqrt{(n+\lambda) P}\right)_i.
$$

Propagate every sigma point through $f$ and $h$ and reassemble the moments:

$$
\hat x_{k|k-1} = \sum_i W^{(i)}_m f(\mathcal{X}^{(i)}_{k-1}), \qquad
P_{k|k-1}     = \sum_i W^{(i)}_c \big(\cdot\big)\big(\cdot\big)^\top + Q.
$$

Captures second-order moment effects exactly for any $f, h$ and sidesteps
Jacobian computation, at the cost of $2n+1$ function evaluations per step.

### Square-Root Filter (SQRT)

Algebraically identical to the standard Kalman filter, but state covariance
is stored as a Cholesky factor $S$ with $P = SS^\top$. The predict / update
steps use QR decompositions to update $S$ directly, guaranteeing
positive-definiteness in finite precision (Bierman 1977; Kaminski, Bryson &
Schmidt 1971):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix} S_{k|k-1} \end{bmatrix}
&= \mathrm{qr}\bigl( [\,T S_{k-1|k-1} \;\; G\,] \bigr) \\
\begin{bmatrix} S_{y,k} & 0 \\ K_k S_{y,k} & S_{k|k} \end{bmatrix}
&= \mathrm{qr}\bigl( [\, R^{1/2} \;\; H S_{k|k-1} \;;\; 0 \;\; S_{k|k-1}\,] \bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Recommended whenever the conventional form repeatedly produces non-PSD
$P_{k|k}$ (e.g. very small $R$, ill-conditioned $H$).

### Information Filter (IF)

Reparameterise in terms of the *information matrix*
$Y_k = P_k^{-1}$ and *information vector* $y_k = P_k^{-1} \hat x_k$. The
update step then becomes purely additive:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y_{k|k} &= Y_{k|k-1} + H^\top R^{-1} H \\
y_{k|k} &= y_{k|k-1} + H^\top R^{-1} z_k.
\end{aligned}
$$

Useful when (i) initial uncertainty is infinite ($Y_0 = 0$ encodes a
diffuse prior cleanly), (ii) sensors arrive asynchronously and updates can
be summed, and (iii) the state dimension is large but observation
information is sparse.

### Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF)

Maintain an ensemble of $N$ state samples
$\{x_k^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N$ instead of an explicit covariance. Propagate each
member through the (possibly nonlinear) $f$, then update each member with a
gain estimated from the ensemble sample covariance and from
*perturbed observations* $y_k + v^{(i)}_k$ (Evensen 1994, 2003):

$$
P_{k|k-1} \approx \frac{1}{N-1} \sum_i (x_k^{(i)} - \bar x)(x_k^{(i)} - \bar x)^\top.
$$

The killer use case is geophysics, where the state dimension ($10^6$ grid
cells) makes a full $P$ unrepresentable but a moderate ensemble
($N \in [50, 500]$) gives usable second-moment information.

## Datasets

The three datasets live under [`data/`](data/) and are produced by the
single script [`generate_nonlinear.py`](data/generate_nonlinear.py). All
generators are reproducible (fixed seed) and return float64 arrays.

### Pendulum (`pendulum.csv`)

Damped pendulum with state $[\theta, \dot\theta]$, integrated with a
symplectic semi-implicit Euler step to keep energy bounded:

$$
\dot\theta_{k+1} = \dot\theta_k - \tfrac{g}{L}\sin\theta_k\,\Delta t + w_k\,\Delta t,
\qquad
\theta_{k+1} = \theta_k + \dot\theta_{k+1}\,\Delta t,
$$

observed via $y_k = \sin\theta_k + v_k$. Default: $n=200$, $\Delta t = 0.1$,
$\sigma_{\text{obs}} = 0.3$.

### Target tracking (`target_tracking.csv`)

Two-dimensional near-constant-velocity target with state $[x, v_x, y, v_y]$
driven by piecewise-constant white-noise acceleration, observed in
range/bearing coordinates from a sensor at the origin:

$$
y_k = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{x_k^2 + y_k^2} \\ \mathrm{atan2}(y_k, x_k) \end{bmatrix} + v_k.
$$

Default: $n=100$, $\Delta t = 1$, $\sigma_{\text{range}}=5$,
$\sigma_{\text{bearing}}=0.05$.

### Lorenz 63 (`lorenz63.csv`)

The canonical chaotic system

$$
\dot x = \sigma (y-x), \qquad \dot y = x(\rho - z) - y, \qquad \dot z = xy - \beta z
$$

integrated by classical RK4, observed on $[x, z]$ with additive Gaussian
noise. Default: $n=500$, $\Delta t = 0.01$, $\sigma_{\text{obs}}=2$.

## Notebooks (added in F6.2–F6.5)

1. **EKF on the pendulum** — derive the Jacobians of the discretised
   pendulum dynamics, run the EKF on the noisy `sin(theta)` observations,
   and compare the recovered angle path against the latent truth.
2. **UKF on target tracking** — implement the unscented transform with
   $(\alpha, \beta, \kappa)$ scaling, apply it to the range/bearing tracker
   and benchmark against the EKF, which is known to bias the bearing
   updates.
3. **EnKF on Lorenz 63** — run the stochastic EnKF with a small
   ($N \approx 50$) ensemble, illustrate covariance localisation, and
   contrast the resulting analysis with the (much more expensive) extended
   filter on the same trajectory.

A combined notebook on the square-root and information filter formulations
shows how to refactor a working linear/EKF filter to gain numerical
stability and decentralised-update structure.

## Validation

Independent replication of the Python results uses:

- **R** (`validation/R/`): the
  [`FKF`](https://cran.r-project.org/package=FKF) and
  [`KFAS`](https://cran.r-project.org/package=KFAS) packages for the linear
  pieces (EKF / SQRT comparisons), and the standalone implementations in
  Sarkka's companion code for the UKF and EnKF.
- **Stata** (`validation/stata/`): limited — Stata does not ship a native
  EKF / UKF, so the Stata cross-checks are restricted to the linearised
  EKF runs via `sspace` with manually supplied Jacobians.
